# Sampling decisions under exact and approximate risk: frozen protocol

Date: 27 October 2026. Version 1. Original synthetic, finite software study.
The v1.0.0 paper, 148-case matrix, frozen sources and old release remain unchanged.
No inference/model calls, private material, external API fees, or field data.

## Question and statistical contract

Does a continuous worst-risk oracle change sampling allocations, and lower their
certified risk, compared with a five-point probability grid? Does its cost
advantage persist against a vectorized reusable conic program? Exact and conic
solve the same continuous problem, so no intrinsic statistical superiority over
a correct conic answer is hypothesized.

Independent binomial strata; known normalized positive weights; counts chosen
before observing any outcomes. An exogenous calendar has unit-energy opportunities,
fixed eligible strata per slot, no storage and no outcome-dependent decisions.
Idle is allowed; all feasible count vectors under the same budget are enumerated.
No inferred correlations, unknown weights, adaptive stopping or claims about real
energy availability are covered. The estimand is a weighted probability, not a
finite-population sample mean. No actual binomial outcomes need to be drawn.

The estimator is fixed by c: a_i=w_i*n_i/(n_i+c) if n_i>0, otherwise a_i=0.
c=0 gives observed-stratum sample means with midpoint treatment of missing strata.
The estimator rule is identical for all evaluators. No tuning of c after results.

## Fresh inputs and budget

Seed 271027117, generated once with no risk-based selection. 48 scenarios:
k in {2,3,4}, c in {0,1/2,1,2}, four calendars each. Raw weights are independent
uniform integers 1..9 normalized to sum one. Calendar replicas have B={2,3,4,4}
powered slots in horizons B+2. Eligible sets are respectively all strata, random
nonempty subsets, random subsets of size k-1, and one fixed stratum throughout.
The final replica is a deliberately restricted-coverage control. Each powered
slot permits idle or one eligible sample; other slots require idle. A DP deduplicates
count vectors and retains the lexicographically first witness schedule. Candidate
sets are frozen with raw calendars; no risk outcomes are generated by the generator.
All-zero counts are included. Published maxima are at most 1,344 unique allocations.

Three complete passes per scenario, same lexicographic candidate order. Each
method evaluates every candidate; maximum 4,032 exact, 4,032 grid and 4,032 conic
evaluations (algebraic cases may avoid conic iterations). The independent exact
face-enumeration reference evaluates each unique allocation once, at most 1,344.
No old evaluation cases are reused. Preflight tests use separate handwritten toys.
One formal queue only, no discarded warmup, retries, adaptive grid refinement,
input replacement or threshold tuning. RUN_GUARD prevents accidental rerunning.
Wall budget 900 seconds after imports, checked before each candidate/method call;
this is not a hard in-call timeout. Conic maximum iterations 200. Stop and preserve
all partial rows/failures when the budget or correctness gate fails.

## Evaluators and fairness

Grid: p={0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1}^k with rational risk calculations. Nonnegative d permits
absolute-value symmetry reduction to x={0,1/2,1}^k, exactly the same grid maximum.
Exact: unchanged rational oracle plus independent weighted-Cauchy certificate check.
Conic: a vectorized parameterized SOCP, DPP verified, one program construction
per scenario, cached across all candidates and three passes, warm_start=True.
No oracle-derived warm start, answer or candidate pruning. Numerical residuals,
warnings, iterations, internal solver time and reported risk are retained.
All receive identical d,v; shared candidate generation/input transformation is
outside method timers. Exact timing includes certificate checking; conic time
includes parameter update, solve and residual audit. Class construction is recorded
separately and added to first-pass batch totals. Imports are excluded for all.
Order rotates through grid/exact/conic, exact/conic/grid and conic/grid/exact
over passes. Pass 1 is cold-to-reused; passes 2 and 3 reuse the entire program.
No universal or cross-machine speed claim; repeats are technical, not new scenarios.

## Decision endpoints and falsifiers

Primary independent unit is scenario, not candidate, pass, stratum or solver call.
Use the first pass for decisions; later passes measure timing and selection stability.
Every exact/grid minimum retains its complete tied set. Shared deterministic tie
rule chooses the first lexicographic count vector. Report selected regret, and best
and worst regret over tied minimizers, relative to the exact minimum over ALL feasible
allocations. A tie-robust gain requires the BEST grid-tied regret to be strictly >0.
A bad lexicographic choice with a correct tied option is only tie-dependent gain.

Conic selection uses only its numerical output: take all accepted values within
1e-7 of its minimum, choosing the first lexicographic candidate; reject a scenario
if any required conic evaluation fails. Do not use exact answers to choose a winner.
Report selected certified regret, full numerical-near-set risk range and whether
the set includes an exact optimum. Numerical near ties alone are not solver defects.
Record all passes, including choice instability and error above 1e-7.

Correctness gate: every exact certificate valid; every unique exact optimum equals
the independently implemented face reference; every grid value <= exact; complete
48 scenarios and frozen hashes unchanged. Any mismatch stops positive claims.
Conic comparison gate additionally requires every conic record accepted with risk
error <=1e-7. A failing numerical comparison is inconclusive, not our victory.

Grid decision support: the correctness gate passes and at least one scenario
has strictly positive best grid-tied regret. This establishes existence only within
the disclosed finite cohort, not population prevalence or significance. If only
selected/worst tied regret is positive, label TIE_DEPENDENT_ONLY. If no grid-tied
option has positive regret, label NO_DECISION_GAIN_OBSERVED. Preserve every outcome.
If the exact/reference gate or completeness fails, label INCONCLUSIVE and state why.
Conic comparison status is separate: a numerical failure cannot erase an exact
grid regret proof, but precludes a successful full three-way comparison and a
speed victory claim on that failed comparison. Retain all conic failures.

Speed is secondary: report per-scenario cold batch conic/exact ratios including
build, and warm ratios for passes 2/3, each ratio over the same entire candidate set.
Report medians/ranges; no statistical confidence claim. A warm median ratio <=1
does not support a speed advantage; >=2 passes the prespecified engineering threshold.
Ratios between 1 and 2 indicate a smaller observed advantage. Do not target 30x.
If exact/grid always select equally good designs, decision improvement is unsupported
in this cohort even if some risk numbers differ. Invalid statistical assumptions or
a maintained equivalent implementation can defeat application/product claims.

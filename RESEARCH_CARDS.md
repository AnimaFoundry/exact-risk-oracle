# Three mechanism-driven research ideas and their falsifiers

Date: 27 October 2026. These directions connect sensing, learning and evidence
verification. A compelling analogy is a starting hypothesis, not proof of a
new invention. Original synthetic mathematical/software work only.

## 1. Energy-correlated missingness and certified sampling value

**Original hypothesis:** reserve one scarce observation for a dark/unobserved
state rather than spend every early energy opportunity on already covered states.
**Strong baseline:** matched-information periodic scheduling, age-of-information
optimization, and exact finite minimax scheduling under the same resource budget.
**Cheap test:** exact finite enumeration of an exogenous synthetic energy calendar.
**Falsifier:** more state coverage fails to lower worst-case estimator risk or is
already matched by a maintained baseline. The earlier finite audit found precisely
this estimator-dependent failure; universal reserve-one improvement was rejected.

**Refined method selected here:** replace unreliable finite-grid risk reassurance
with an exact continuous-risk value and independently checkable adversarial witness.
This refinement is a different mathematical question, with newly frozen inputs;
it does not relabel the old negative result as a successful policy.
**Independent value tested:** exact certificate output with low one-shot overhead
versus a general maintained conic formulation. Correctness and the prespecified
finite operational gate passed; broad novelty and real design improvement remain
unestablished. See paper, protocol and complete saved results.
**Further falsifier:** an equivalent earlier specialization defeats algorithmic
novelty; no changed/better design choices defeats a decision-level value claim;
invalid statistical assumptions defeat the claimed application.

## 2. Crossmodal bridge selection under shared failures

**Hypothesis:** sensor channels with complementary observability need a small set
of shared calibration events; select bridges that survive a common power-domain
failure instead of maximizing pair count.
**Cheap test if pursued:** original small graphs and linear latent variables,
with fixed bridge budget, shared failure groups and exact observability targets.
**Strong baselines:** spanning structures, fault-tolerant spanners, existing
optimal experimental design and row-space observability certificates.
**Falsifier:** the proposed mechanism is exactly a known graph/design method, or
advantages disappear when baseline information and failure assumptions match.
**Current state:** primary screening found close color-fault-tolerant and
calibration/identifiability antecedents. No distinct method was established.
No new bridge experiment or product is claimed; this branch was not selected.

## 3. Evidence auditing with hidden common ancestry

**Hypothesis:** ten agreeing citations may inherit one wrong source; spend a
limited verification budget on observations that remove conclusion ambiguity,
rather than simply count citations.
**Cheap test if pursued:** bounded original finite possible-world sets, trusted
fact probes, explicit hidden-copy limits and a budget of zero to three probes.
**Strong baselines:** information-value source validation, active diagnosis,
generalized binary search, and an exact minimax decision tree on the same set.
**Falsifier:** the algorithm reduces to an existing decision-tree recurrence,
gets extra ancestry information, or lacks a guarantee when its uncertainty set is
violated. Correctness on an assumed world set does not establish real provenance.
**Current state:** known provenance, data-fusion validation and decision-tree
methods already cover the broad mechanism. No independently valuable algorithm
was established. No experiment or new product is claimed for this branch.
